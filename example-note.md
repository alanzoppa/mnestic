# Lunar regolith composting experiment — batch 47

## Objective

Test whether Martian thermophilic bacteria can sustain decomposition cycles in Lunar regolith substrate under reduced gravity conditions. This is a follow-up to batch 44, which showed promising pH stabilization after 72 hours but collapsed at the 96-hour mark.

## Procedure

1. Prepare 3 substrate trays with variable perchlorate concentrations (0.5%, 0.8%, 1.2%)
2. Inoculate with *Bacillus martiensis* strain MK-7 (courtesy of Dr. Vasquez's lab at Zeta Base)
3. Maintain ambient temperature at 38°C with humidity cycling between 40–60% RH
4. Log pH, ammonia output, and colony density every 4 hours

## Observations — Day 1

Tomás noted the 0.5% perchlorate tray showed immediate bacterial adhesion to regolith particles — much faster than Earth-baseline runs. The reduced gravity appears to improve substrate penetration. Priya flagged a potential contamination issue in tray 3 (1.2%) — some particulate residue from the previous batch wasn't fully cleaned from the sensor housing. We decided to proceed and note it as a variable.

Ammonia levels in tray 1 at the 12-hour mark: **0.34 ppm** (Earth baseline: 0.21 ppm). Promising.

## Observations — Day 3

pH has stabilized at 6.8 in trays 1 and 2. Tray 3 remains erratic, fluctuating between 5.1 and 7.4 — likely the contamination effect. Kira suspects the perchlorate concentration is inhibiting the buffering capacity.

Colony density is above threshold in all trays. The colony morphology under Lunar conditions is notably different — more filamentous, less spherical clustering. I've requested imaging time on the Zeta Base electron microscope for next cycle.
